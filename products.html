The collections

Nothing here started as raw material. Five waste streams, five collections, each one recovered, processed and remade in Bangladesh. Prices are introductory, in Bangladeshi Taka.

Textile Paper Construction Organic Metal
01

Recovered textile offcuts

The Textile Collection

Bangladesh cuts more fabric than almost anywhere on earth, and the offcuts too small for a garment are thrown out by the tonne. We wash them, grade them by colour, and build them into things people carry every day.

The Sathi

Recovered textile offcuts

Sathi means companion. Two main compartments, a padded laptop sleeve and straps built for a student who carries everything, every day, for years.

Haat Toteimages/haat-tote.jpg
Tote bag

The Haat

৳399

Recovered cotton, canvas, denim

Named for the local market. Strong handles, an inner pocket and a shape that swallows a week of shopping, so nobody needs another plastic bag.

Chhaya Sleeveimages/chhaya-sleeve.jpg
Laptop sleeve

The Chhaya

৳599

Recovered textile, soft padding

Chhaya means shelter. Padded on both faces with a front pocket for a charger, because the thing that protects a laptop should not itself cost a fortune.

02

Recovered waste paper

The Paper Collection

Offices, schools and print shops throw out paper by the sackful. We collect it, pulp it and press it back into sheets, so the same fibre goes round again instead of a tree coming down.

Bhor Notebookimages/bhor-notebook.jpg
Notebook

The Bhor

৳149

Recycled paper, fabric cover

Bhor means dawn, which is where Shurjo takes its own name from. Recycled pages bound inside a cover cut from textile offcuts, so one notebook uses two waste streams.

Ankur Cardimages/ankur-card.jpg
Seed paper card

The Ankur

৳99

Plantable seed paper

Ankur means sprout. Wildflower seeds are pressed into the pulp, so once the card has been read it can be soaked, planted and grown. The only greeting card that does not end in a bin.

Ashroy Boximages/ashroy-box.jpg
Packaging box

The Ashroy

৳59 to ৳149 by size

Recovered corrugated board

Ashroy means shelter. Custom sized boxes for businesses that want packaging to stop being the most wasteful part of what they sell. Printed with your own branding.

03

Construction and demolition waste

The Construction Collection

Dhaka builds and demolishes constantly, and the rubble goes to landfill. Crushed and remoulded, it becomes the pavements, tiles and benches the same city is short of.

Poth Paverimages/poth-paver.jpg
Paving block

The Poth

৳30 to ৳35 per sq ft

Crushed concrete and stone waste

Poth means path. Interlocking blocks for footpaths, driveways and school yards, priced under the market because the raw material was headed for a dump anyway.

Nokshi Tileimages/nokshi-tile.jpg
Terrazzo tile

The Nokshi

৳70 to ৳120 per sq ft

Marble and ceramic waste chips

Nokshi means patterned, after the embroidered kantha quilts. The speckle in every tile records exactly what came through the yard that week, so no two batches match.

Bishram Benchimages/bishram-bench.jpg
Public bench

The Bishram

৳3,500 to ৳5,000

Recycled concrete and aggregate

Bishram means rest. Cast for parks, campuses and bus stops. Weather resistant, near impossible to vandalise, and made from the rubble of buildings that used to stand nearby.

04

Household and market organic waste

The Organic Collection

Food waste is the largest share of what Bangladesh throws away and the easiest to turn back into something valuable. Composted properly, it goes straight back to the farms it came from.

Sonali Matiimages/sonali-mati.jpg
Vermicompost

Sonali Mati

৳35 per kg

Composted organic waste

Golden soil. Broken down by worms rather than chemicals, which is slower but produces a fertiliser rich enough that farmers come back for it. Sold loose or in 10kg sacks.

Amrit Tonicimages/amrit-tonic.jpg
Liquid fertiliser

The Amrit

৳100 per litre

Fermented compost extract

Amrit is the old word for the nectar of life. A living feed packed with microorganisms, diluted and watered in. Works on a balcony pot as well as a field.

Chara Potimages/chara-pot.jpg
Plant pot

The Chara

৳80 to ৳150 by size

Pressed organic waste fibre

Chara means seedling. Plant the pot and all, and it breaks down into the soil as the roots grow through it, so there is no transplant shock and no plastic left behind.

05

Scrap and salvaged metal

The Metal Collection

Metal loses nothing when it is melted down, and Bangladesh already has a scrap trade. We take what that trade discards and hand it to fabricators who make things worth keeping.

Thikana Organiserimages/thikana-organiser.jpg
Desk organiser

The Thikana

৳399

Punched scrap steel

Thikana means address, a place where something belongs. Four compartments, powder coated so it will not rust, sized for the clutter that collects on every desk.

Jonaki Lampimages/jonaki-lamp.jpg
Lamp shade

The Jonaki

৳900

Perforated salvaged metal

Jonaki means firefly. The shade is hand punched, so switched on it throws a field of small lights across the ceiling. The pattern is different on every one.

Boithok Chairimages/boithok-chair.jpg
Chair

The Boithok

৳1,800

Recycled steel frame

Boithok is the room where people gather. Stackable, rust treated and light enough to move one handed, built for tea stalls, classrooms and courtyards.
